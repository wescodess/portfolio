# Professional Portfolio Prototype

## Question

Which information architecture and visual direction makes Wesley's portfolio feel most credible, senior, clear, and memorable without relying on personal photography?

This branch is a throwaway design lab. It does not change the production homepage or Sanity model.

## Recommended structure

1. **Persistent navigation** — Work, Experience, About, Contact, and a restrained Bitmoji wordmark.
2. **Positioning hero** — role, the product problems Wesley solves, location/availability, and two useful actions.
3. **Proof strip** — current role, years/domains, product scope, and delivery strengths without invented metrics.
4. **Featured work** — Budds, CharterXE, and WACS, each framed as context → ownership → decisions → outcome.
5. **Experience** — a compact career timeline that establishes progression and scope.
6. **Capabilities** — grouped by engineering outcomes such as product architecture, frontend systems, AI workflows, and delivery quality; technologies support the evidence instead of becoming the story.
7. **Working principles / about** — one concise human section combining philosophy and collaboration style.
8. **Contact** — direct email/CV/social routes first; an optional short form second.

## Remove or merge from the current homepage

- Remove the hero portrait, philosophy photograph, about portrait, and footer portrait.
- Remove the generic engineering-quote marquee; it does not prove seniority.
- Replace the framework-logo wall with capability groups attached to real project evidence.
- Merge Philosophy, Skillset, and About into one focused "How I work" section.
- Move selected work before the long experience/skills sequence.
- Reduce the intro overlay, sticky section stacking, card tilt, and ambient effects; reserve motion for orientation and feedback.
- Use Allrox as an accent at most, with Aeonik or a neutral sans for the main reading experience.

## Five directions

- **A — Executive Editorial:** high-trust narrative and selective proof.
- **B — Product Systems:** architecture, product surfaces, and engineering evidence.
- **C — Case-study Immersive:** project chapters dominate the journey.
- **D — Swiss Résumé:** rigorous recruiter scan and career progression.
- **E — Quiet Studio:** spacious, premium, human, with restrained Bitmoji personality.

## Evaluation rubric

Score each direction from 1–5 on:

- Ten-second clarity: role, seniority, and strongest work are immediately obvious.
- Evidence: ownership and engineering decisions are more prominent than tool lists.
- Trust: claims feel specific, restrained, and defensible.
- Navigation: a recruiter can reach work, experience, CV, and contact without searching.
- Reading comfort: typography, density, contrast, and mobile hierarchy feel effortless.
- Memorability: the portfolio has a recognizable point of view without feeling playful or gimmicky.

The winning implementation may combine sections from multiple directions. The prototypes themselves should remain on this branch and not be promoted directly to production.
